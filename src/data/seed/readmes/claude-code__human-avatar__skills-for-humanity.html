See it think — worked examples

Why social media makes discourse worse despite everyone wanting better

Platforms want healthier discourse. Users say they want it. It keeps getting worse. /s4h-systems-feedback-mapping finds the reinforcing loop: engagement-optimised feeds reward content that provokes reaction, which selects for users who engage with provocation, which trains the algorithm to serve more of it. The stated goal and the actual incentive structure point in opposite directions. No individual decision made it this way — the system produced it.

The ethics and game theory of automating 40 jobs

Your company is considering automating a function that currently employs 40 people. /s4h-ethics-council runs five frameworks and surfaces a genuine conflict: utilitarian logic favours the automation, Kantian reasoning flags that treating displaced workers as means rather than ends requires more than an efficiency argument. /s4h-game-theory-mechanism-design then helps design a transition that aligns incentives — rather than announcing a decision and managing the fallout.

Why a new pricing model keeps failing despite making economic sense

The economics look clear: the new pricing model captures more value, aligns incentives, and is objectively fairer. It keeps getting rejected. /s4h-economics-incentive-mapping finds the problem: the people who must approve it face short-term losses even though the organisation wins long-term. /s4h-economics-coordination then diagnoses the collective action failure — each approver is waiting for someone else to absorb the transition cost first.

Why a product keeps winning technically and losing commercially

Engineers ship a product that outperforms competitors on every measurable dimension. It keeps underperforming in the market. /s4h-design-user-needs finds the gap: the features being optimised are the ones engineers care about, not the ones that determine adoption decisions. /s4h-cognition-mental-models then surfaces the mental model users bring to the product — and shows the product is being built against a different model than the one users actually hold.

Why a platform grows explosively then stalls at the same threshold

A consumer app hit 100,000 users fast, then plateaued. Growth tactics aren't working. /s4h-network-effects diagnoses the type of network effect at play — local, not global — which means the platform reaches saturation within clusters long before it reaches market saturation. /s4h-network-weak-ties then identifies the bridge connections that could carry growth across cluster boundaries.

What a startup's fundraising pitch is actually communicating

A founder's pitch deck is clear and well-structured. Investors keep passing without giving substantive reasons. /s4h-linguistics-framing finds the issue: the deck frames the company as solving a problem investors already think is solved, which pre-loads skepticism before a single number is scrutinised. The facts haven't changed — the frame has been working against the argument the whole time.

The political landscape through game theory

The current political climate feels like everyone is acting against their own interests. /s4h-game-theory-iterated maps the repeated-game structure: parties face short-term defection incentives even when cooperation would benefit everyone long-run, and a shrinking shadow of the future (declining expected gains from future cooperation) explains why moderation keeps losing even when most people say they want it. The equilibrium is stable — not because of irrationality, but because of it.

Whether the current wave of nationalism is unprecedented

The political nationalism of the 2020s feels like something new. /s4h-historical-cycle-detection finds four structural analogues — late-19th-century nativist movements, 1930s economic retrenchment, post-colonial identity politics, 1970s stagflation politics — and maps where the current situation matches the pattern and where it doesn't. More useful than "this has happened before" or "this time is different."

A career pivot with a closing window

You're considering leaving a stable senior role for an early-stage company. The question feels like yes or no. /s4h-strategy-positioning maps how your options change if you wait two years versus going now. /s4h-decision-reversibility-analysis finds the move is partially reversible in the medium term — but with a specific window that closes as you get further from hands-on execution. The pairing surfaces a time constraint neither skill would have found alone.

Whether a dominant tech company's position is actually durable

The company looks unassailable: massive market share, capital, talent. /s4h-ecology-keystone-species maps the dependencies — which other players rely on it and which the platform itself relies on — and finds two suppliers with quietly disproportionate leverage. /s4h-ecology-succession then models what the ecosystem looks like in the next developmental stage, and whether the current dominant player is positioned for it or optimised for conditions that are already passing.

How a species analogy reveals the flaw in a market entry strategy

A company plans to enter a mature market by doing what the market leader does, but cheaper. /s4h-evolution-fitness-landscape maps the landscape: the incumbent occupies a local optimum that is genuinely hard to displace incrementally — competing on the same dimensions means fighting on the incumbent's terms. The analysis points toward a different peak entirely: a niche the incumbent has structurally abandoned.

The hidden cost in every information-rich environment

A team drowns in data but keeps making the same avoidable mistakes. /s4h-information-signal-noise finds that the most decision-relevant signals are buried in low-frequency, low-urgency reports that nobody reads — while high-frequency, high-urgency signals (email, Slack, dashboards) carry almost no information about the actual problem. The team isn't uninformed; it's overloaded with noise while the signal sits unread.

Where to reach for what

Creative work — /s4h-creativity-brainstorm for multi-method exploration, /s4h-constraint-rule-inversion to make limits generative, /s4h-aesthetic-elegance-testing to find what's unnecessary.

Communication — /s4h-communication-objection-mapping to anticipate resistance, /s4h-narrative-tension-mapping to find what earns attention, /s4h-writing-rhetoric to audit what a piece is actually doing.

Full skills reference

Think Sharper

/s4h-logic

  • /s4h-logic — Entry point for the logic toolkit.
  • /s4h-logic-argument-validation — Check whether an argument's premises actually support its conclusion, and identify logical fallacies.
  • /s4h-logic-causality-mapping — Map causal relationships, trace dependencies, and reason about consequences before acting.
  • /s4h-logic-check — A fast, comprehensive logic report on any argument, plan, or reasoning — validates premises, tests inference, detects fallacies, surfaces hidden assumptions, and produces a verdict.
  • /s4h-logic-consistency-check — Surface internal contradictions, conflicting requirements, and edge cases that expose hidden conflicts in a document, spec, plan, design, or set of requirements.
  • /s4h-logic-constraint-mapping — Map the full constraint landscape for a decision, design, or plan — distinguishing hard limits from soft preferences, surfacing hidden constraints, and finding conflicts between them.
  • /s4h-logic-council — Run a reasoning problem, argument, plan, or decision through a council of 5 logical reasoning advisors who analyze it from distinct reasoning frameworks, peer-review each other, and synthesize a verdict on whether the reasoning holds.

/s4h-probability

  • /s4h-probability — Entry point for the probability toolkit.
  • /s4h-probability-base-rate-anchoring — Anchors estimates in historical base rates before adjusting for specific factors.
  • /s4h-probability-confidence-calibration — Tests whether stated confidence levels match available evidence — catching overconfidence and underconfidence.
  • /s4h-probability-expected-value-calculation — Calculates expected value to compare options under uncertainty.
  • /s4h-probability-scenario-weighting — Assigns explicit probabilities to distinct scenarios before making a decision.

/s4h-decision

  • /s4h-decision — Entry point for the decision toolkit.
  • /s4h-decision-criteria-weighting — Runs a weighted multi-criteria analysis — making explicit what matters, how much, and how each option performs against it.
  • /s4h-decision-option-mapping — Ensures all real options are visible before choosing — countering the false dichotomy that limits consideration to the first two options that came to mind.
  • /s4h-decision-premortem-analysis — Imagines the decision has been made and failed — then diagnoses why.
  • /s4h-decision-reversibility-analysis — Categorises a decision by reversibility and applies the appropriate level of process rigour.

/s4h-constraint

  • /s4h-constraint — Entry point for the constraint toolkit.
  • /s4h-constraint-hardness-testing — Tests whether a stated constraint is real — distinguishing genuine limits from assumptions, habits, or politics dressed as facts.
  • /s4h-constraint-rule-inversion — Flips a constraint into a creative driver — uses the limit as the generative force rather than working around it.
  • /s4h-constraint-scope-reduction — Finds the minimum that satisfies the actual requirement — stripping everything wanted but not needed.
  • /s4h-constraint-workaround-mapping — Finds paths around a fixed constraint without removing it — routing around a hard limit to reach the same goal.

/s4h-game-theory

  • /s4h-game-theory — Routes to the right game-theory skill for your strategic situation.
  • /s4h-game-theory-auction — Analyses bidding strategy and auction design — how much to bid, how to avoid the winner's curse, and how to design revenue-maximising or efficient auctions.
  • /s4h-game-theory-coalition — Analyses which coalitions form and how to divide gains fairly using cooperative game theory and the Shapley value.
  • /s4h-game-theory-equilibrium — Finds the stable outcome of a strategic interaction — the point where no player can improve their result by changing their strategy alone.
  • /s4h-game-theory-iterated — Analyses long-run repeated interactions — how cooperation forms, how trust is built, how defection spirals start, and which strategies sustain cooperation.
  • /s4h-game-theory-mechanism-design — Designs rules and incentive systems that produce desired outcomes even when players are self-interested.
  • /s4h-game-theory-prisoners-dilemma — Analyses cooperation problems where individual rationality produces collective irrationality.
  • /s4h-game-theory-signaling — Analyses credibility problems and designs signals that are believable because they're costly to fake.

/s4h-epistemology

  • /s4h-epistemology — Entry point for the epistemology toolkit.
  • /s4h-epistemology-epistemic-status — Produces an honest, rigorous calibration of what you know vs. believe vs. assume vs. hope across a domain.
  • /s4h-epistemology-justification — Analyzes what would actually justify believing a claim.
  • /s4h-epistemology-knowledge-types — Maps what kind of knowing is actually in play for a claim or question.
  • /s4h-epistemology-limits — Identifies what can't be known and why, then clarifies what can be established within those limits and reframes the question into its answerable part.

/s4h-investigation

  • /s4h-investigation — Entry point for the investigation toolkit.
  • /s4h-investigation-claim-decomposition — Breaks a complex claim into its smallest independently verifiable parts, classifies each sub-claim, and identifies which parts carry the most logical load.
  • /s4h-investigation-counter-hypothesis — Generates the best alternative explanations for the same observations.
  • /s4h-investigation-evidence-audit — Evaluates the quality, strength, and completeness of evidence for a claim.
  • /s4h-investigation-source-trace — Traces a claim back to its origin: who first made it, what evidence it rested on, and how it has been distorted in transmission.
  • /s4h-investigation-triangulation — Verifies a claim across genuinely independent sources, distinguishing amplification from true corroboration.

/s4h-economics

  • /s4h-economics — Entry point for the economics toolkit.
  • /s4h-economics-opportunity-cost — Maps what you give up by choosing X — because every choice forecloses alternatives, and the invisible cost is often the decisive one.
  • /s4h-economics-incentive-mapping — Maps who benefits, who pays, and what behaviors the incentive structure produces.
  • /s4h-economics-externalities — Identifies costs and benefits not borne by the actor — and whether they should be internalized.
  • /s4h-economics-margin — Applies marginal thinking: the relevant decision is always the next unit, not the average, and sunk costs are irrelevant.
  • /s4h-economics-coordination — Diagnoses collective action failures and public goods problems — why individually rational behavior produces collectively irrational outcomes.

/s4h-information

  • /s4h-information — Entry point for the information toolkit.
  • /s4h-information-signal-noise — Separates meaningful signal from irrelevant noise in any dataset, communication, or situation — identifying what carries actual information and what is static.
  • /s4h-information-compression — Finds the shortest lossless representation of a message or model — the minimum description that preserves what matters.
  • /s4h-information-redundancy — Identifies where redundancy is protective versus wasteful — and whether removing it creates brittleness or just bloat.
  • /s4h-information-entropy — Measures how much genuine uncertainty or surprise is in a source — and what that implies for decisions that depend on it.

/s4h-cognition

  • /s4h-cognition — Entry point for the cognition toolkit.
  • /s4h-cognition-attention — Maps what is and isn't receiving cognitive attention in a situation — and whether the right things are being prioritised.
  • /s4h-cognition-mental-models — Surfaces the mental models driving a decision or interpretation — and tests whether they fit the actual situation.
  • /s4h-cognition-metacognition — Examines how you are thinking, not just what you are thinking — catching reasoning errors before they compound.
  • /s4h-cognition-cognitive-load — Identifies where cognitive load is exceeding capacity — and redesigns the task, environment, or communication to work within limits.

/s4h-play

  • /s4h-play-stimulus-generation — Introduces a random, unrelated element to break mental fixation — forcing new associations that bypass the groove of familiar thinking.
  • /s4h-play-worst-case-reversal — Deliberately designs the worst possible version — then reverses each failure mode into a design principle.

/s4h-evolution

  • /s4h-evolution — Entry point for the evolution toolkit.

  • /s4h-evolution-fitness-landscape — Models the fitness landscape for a strategy or design — mapping peaks, valleys, and the paths between them to find whether local optima are trapping progress.
  • /s4h-evolution-arms-race — Diagnoses co-evolutionary escalation dynamics — where two parties keep adapting to each other in a cycle with no stable end.

/s4h-design

  • /s4h-design — Entry point for the design toolkit.
  • /s4h-design-user-needs — Surfaces what users actually need as distinct from what they ask for — the gap between stated preference and revealed behavior is where design fails.
  • /s4h-design-constraints — Maps the full constraint space for a design problem — material, technical, human, contextual — and finds where constraints are generative rather than limiting.
  • /s4h-design-iteration — Structures a rapid iteration cycle — hypothesis, prototype, test, learn — to get to better solutions faster by failing informatively rather than expensively.
  • /s4h-design-simplicity — Finds the simplest version of a design that fully solves the problem — removing everything that doesn't earn its place without losing what makes it work.

/s4h-linguistics

  • /s4h-linguistics — Entry point for the linguistics toolkit.
  • /s4h-linguistics-framing — Identifies the frame embedded in a word, phrase, or question — and how that frame pre-loads the answer before reasoning begins.
  • /s4h-linguistics-connotation — Maps the emotional and associative load of word choices — the difference between what a word denotes and what it implies.
  • /s4h-linguistics-pragmatics — Analyses what is being communicated beyond the literal meaning — implicature, speech acts, and the gap between what is said and what is meant.
  • /s4h-linguistics-semantic-drift — Tracks how a word or concept has shifted meaning over time or across communities — and whether the current argument depends on conflating distinct meanings.

Think in Time & Systems

/s4h-systems

  • /s4h-systems — Entry point for the systems thinking toolkit.
  • /s4h-systems-archetype-matching — Applies the 8 classic system archetypes (Senge) to diagnose recurring system behavior.
  • /s4h-systems-emergence-detection — Identifies system-level properties that exist nowhere in any individual component.
  • /s4h-systems-feedback-mapping — Identifies all reinforcing (+) and balancing (−) feedback loops in a system.
  • /s4h-systems-leverage-analysis — Finds where small interventions produce large, lasting change using Donella Meadows' leverage point hierarchy.

/s4h-temporal

  • /s4h-temporal — Entry point for the temporal thinking toolkit.
  • /s4h-temporal-cycle-detection — Identifies what recurring cycle a situation is an instance of and where in that cycle you currently are.
  • /s4h-temporal-futures-mapping — Explores possible, probable, and preferable futures using scenario thinking.
  • /s4h-temporal-horizon-mapping — Maps consequences of a decision across short, medium, and long time horizons.
  • /s4h-temporal-timing-analysis — Assesses whether now is the right time to act, wait, or prepare.

/s4h-historical

  • /s4h-historical — Entry point for the historical reasoning toolkit.
  • /s4h-historical-cycle-detection — Identifies what recurring cycle the current situation is an instance of — and where in that cycle you currently are.
  • /s4h-historical-failure-analysis — Extracts recurring failure modes from similar past situations — most failures have happened before in recognisable patterns.
  • /s4h-historical-lesson-extraction — Extracts the transferable principle from a specific historical case — separating the contingent surface details from the underlying rule that applies across contexts.
  • /s4h-historical-precedent-analysis — Finds and applies genuinely similar historical situations to inform a current decision — distinguishing true precedents from superficial analogies.

/s4h-resource

  • /s4h-resource — Entry point for the resource toolkit.
  • /s4h-resource-allocation-analysis — Distributes limited resources across competing needs — making the trade-offs explicit rather than implicit.
  • /s4h-resource-bottleneck-analysis — Identifies what is actually constraining throughput — using Theory of Constraints logic: the system can only move as fast as its slowest point.
  • /s4h-resource-leverage-mapping — Finds the highest-leverage use of available resources — where the same input produces the most output.
  • /s4h-resource-waste-audit — Finds where resources are being lost, duplicated, or underused — the seven wastes applied to knowledge work.

/s4h-strategy

  • /s4h-strategy — Routes to the right strategy skill for any adversarial or competitive reasoning situation.
  • /s4h-strategy-alliance — Maps parties, identifies natural allies and swing parties, and assesses alliance stability for coalition-building in competitive contexts.
  • /s4h-strategy-deception — Manages information asymmetry in legitimate competitive contexts — what to protect, what impressions work in your favor, and what your opponent may be concealing from you.
  • /s4h-strategy-force-economy — Finds the minimum intervention that achieves the objective — especially when you're outgunned or under-resourced.
  • /s4h-strategy-intelligence — Audits what you actually know vs. what you're assuming about yourself and your opponent before acting.
  • /s4h-strategy-positioning — Builds the conditions for competitive unassailability before the contest begins.
  • /s4h-strategy-terrain — Maps the competitive landscape to identify where you have advantage, where contests are evenly matched, and where engagement is costly.
  • /s4h-strategy-timing — Analyzes whether to act now or wait, reads your opponent's rhythm, and identifies trigger conditions for the right moment.
  • /s4h-strategy-victory — Defines what winning actually means before the contest begins — prevents the pyrrhic trap of winning in ways that lose the larger goal.

/s4h-network

  • /s4h-network — Entry point for the network thinking toolkit.
  • /s4h-network-centrality — Identifies the most influential nodes in a network — the people, ideas, or components whose removal or activation disproportionately affects the whole.
  • /s4h-network-contagion — Models how things spread through a network — ideas, behaviors, failures, diseases — and where to intervene to accelerate or arrest spread.
  • /s4h-network-weak-ties — Maps the weak ties in a network and identifies where they provide access to information, opportunities, or bridges unavailable through strong ties alone.
  • /s4h-network-effects — Diagnoses whether a product, platform, or system exhibits network effects — and what type — to assess whether value compounds with scale or saturates.

/s4h-ecology

  • /s4h-ecology — Entry point for the ecology toolkit.
  • /s4h-ecology-carrying-capacity — Maps the limits a system can sustain — identifying where growth will hit a ceiling and what determines that ceiling.
  • /s4h-ecology-keystone-species — Identifies the keystone element in a system — the component whose removal triggers disproportionate collapse across the whole.
  • /s4h-ecology-interdependence — Maps the web of mutual dependencies in a system — finding the hidden relationships that make apparently independent elements fragile when separated.
  • /s4h-ecology-succession — Maps the succession dynamics in a system — how it moves through stages of development, what triggers each transition, and what a mature stable state looks like.

See More Clearly

/s4h-aesthetic

  • /s4h-aesthetic — Entry point for the aesthetic toolkit.
  • /s4h-aesthetic-coherence-check — Tests whether the parts of something form a unified whole — finding the jarring inconsistencies that accumulate when different contributors work without a shared vision.
  • /s4h-aesthetic-elegance-testing — Tests whether a solution is more complex than it needs to be — distinguishing necessary complexity from accidental complexity that accreted over time.
  • /s4h-aesthetic-pattern-detection — Identifies the underlying formal pattern at work — because most successful designs, arguments, and solutions share deep structural patterns, and naming the pattern unlocks the playbook.
  • /s4h-aesthetic-simplicity-analysis — Finds the simpler version while preserving what matters — not arbitrary reduction, but finding the core and discarding what is not it.

/s4h-sensory

  • /s4h-sensory — Entry point for the sensory observation toolkit.
  • /s4h-sensory-detail-mining — Finds specific details being overlooked — the most important information is often present but not being registered.
  • /s4h-sensory-signal-detection — Separates meaningful signal from background noise — finding what actually matters among everything present.
  • /s4h-sensory-structured-observation — Applies disciplined observation to a situation — suspending interpretation to see what's actually there before deciding what it means.
Quick examples — one per domain

/s4h-logic

You believe working from home makes you more productive because you get more done on those days. /s4h-logic-check finds the flaw: you only work from home when you have focused work scheduled anyway — the variable you think is causing the effect is just correlated with it.

/s4h-probability

A doctor tells you a test for a rare disease is 99% accurate and you tested positive. /s4h-probability-base-rate-anchoring shows why you're probably still fine: when the disease affects 1 in 10,000 people, even a highly accurate test produces mostly false positives.

/s4h-decision

You've been offered a chance to move abroad for a year. It feels like a binary yes/no. /s4h-decision-option-mapping surfaces four options you weren't considering: go for six months, negotiate remote work, defer by a year, or go with a return clause.

/s4h-constraint

You want to write a novel but insist you have no time. /s4h-constraint-hardness-testing asks whether the constraint is real — and finds it's a habit dressed as a fact. The actual constraint is that you haven't protected a 45-minute slot.

/s4h-game-theory

You and a housemate both hate cleaning the kitchen but neither wants to go first. /s4h-game-theory-prisoners-dilemma shows why this equilibrium is stable and what actually breaks it: not goodwill, but a credible commitment mechanism.

/s4h-epistemology

Everyone in your social circle agrees on a contested topic and it feels like obvious truth. /s4h-epistemology-epistemic-status maps how much of your belief rests on actual evidence versus social contagion — and which parts would survive if your environment were different.

/s4h-investigation

You've heard that people only use 10% of their brain. /s4h-investigation-source-trace traces the claim back to its supposed origin and finds no credible scientific source — it appears to have emerged from a misquote and spread through repetition.

/s4h-economics

Your company is subsidising a service that everyone uses but nobody values. /s4h-economics-opportunity-cost maps what that budget forecloses — and finds the invisible cost is three times larger than the visible one.

/s4h-information

Your team produces weekly status reports that nobody reads. /s4h-information-entropy measures how much genuine new information each report contains — and finds it's near zero, because everything in the report was already known by the people receiving it.

/s4h-cognition

A senior engineer keeps making the same class of architecture mistake across different projects. /s4h-cognition-mental-models surfaces the model driving the decisions — and finds it's a model built for a context that no longer applies.

/s4h-creativity

You've been writing the same kind of music for three years and everything sounds the same. /s4h-creativity-lateral-thinking forces you to approach the next track as if you were scoring a silent film — and the constraint breaks the pattern.

/s4h-analogy

You're struggling to explain how machine learning works to someone with no technical background. /s4h-analogy-domain-transfer imports the structure of learning to ride a bike: you don't study the physics, you fall, adjust, and eventually your body just knows.

/s4h-play

Your short story feels predictable but you can't see where. /s4h-play-worst-case-reversal asks you to design the most boring possible version of the story — and the list of failure modes reveals exactly what the current draft is doing.

/s4h-evolution

A competitor keeps copying your product features and you keep losing the advantage. /s4h-evolution-arms-race maps the escalation dynamic — and finds you're both locked into a cycle neither side can exit unilaterally, optimising for a dimension that neither side actually wants to compete on.

/s4h-design

You've shipped five versions of a feature and users still find it confusing. /s4h-design-user-needs separates what users asked for from what they actually need — and finds the confusion is downstream of a mismatch between the feature's mental model and the user's.

/s4h-communication

You sent a message that was meant to be supportive and the other person felt criticised. /s4h-communication-clarity-audit finds the two sentences that read as judgement to the receiver even though they weren't written that way.

/s4h-social

A group project has stalled and nobody can explain why. /s4h-social-dynamics-analysis identifies that one person holds informal veto power over decisions and everyone else is routing around them without naming it.

/s4h-emotional

A close friend keeps cancelling plans at the last minute. /s4h-emotional-resistance-diagnosis maps what might be underneath the behaviour — not flakiness, but something they're avoiding that the plans surface.

/s4h-ethics

You found someone's wallet containing a significant amount of cash and no ID. /s4h-ethics-council walks through five ethical frameworks and finds they mostly converge — with one genuinely dissenting position that's worth taking seriously.

/s4h-identity

You keep agreeing to commitments that make you miserable. /s4h-identity-values-clarification surfaces the gap between what you say you value and what your decisions actually reveal — and names the value that's been running your choices without your endorsement.

/s4h-narrative

You're giving a speech that matters to you but it keeps landing flat in rehearsal. /s4h-narrative-tension-mapping finds that you've removed all the stakes in an attempt to sound measured — and that's exactly what's killing it.

/s4h-psychology

You've tried to quit a habit five times and it keeps returning. /s4h-psychology-behavior-change diagnoses what's maintaining it: not lack of willpower, but a specific trigger-routine-reward loop that none of your previous attempts addressed.

/s4h-mindset

You've been dreading a difficult conversation for three weeks and it's getting worse. /s4h-mindset-stoic separates what's in your control from what isn't — and finds the avoidance is costing more than the conversation would.

/s4h-writing

Your essay makes a strong argument but readers say it doesn't land. /s4h-writing-rhetoric reveals the structure leads with the conclusion before earning it — the reader hasn't been brought along, so the argument feels asserted rather than demonstrated.

/s4h-linguistics

Your policy proposal keeps getting mischaracterised in discussion. /s4h-linguistics-framing finds the word you chose to describe the mechanism carries a connotation that pre-loads opposition — a different word, identical in denotation, removes the friction.

/s4h-systems

Every time your team fixes one problem, two more appear. /s4h-systems-feedback-mapping finds the reinforcing loop: the fixes are creating conditions that generate the next set of problems faster than the team can resolve them.

/s4h-temporal

You're deciding whether to go back to study and the short-term cost feels prohibitive. /s4h-temporal-horizon-mapping maps the decision at one year, five years, and twenty — and shows that the framing that makes it look impossible only holds at the shortest horizon.

/s4h-historical

You're starting an ambitious creative project and feel pressure to have a breakthrough immediately. /s4h-historical-failure-analysis maps how most significant creative works actually developed: slowly, with long unproductive stretches that looked like failure until they weren't.

/s4h-resource

You have three hours to prepare for an important conversation and you're trying to prepare for everything. /s4h-resource-bottleneck-analysis finds the real constraint: there are two specific questions you can't answer and everything else is already solid.

/s4h-strategy

You want to win a local chess tournament. /s4h-strategy-positioning shows that the decisive work happens before the match: in the openings you prepare, the players you study, and the psychological state you arrive in.

/s4h-network

You've been trying to break into a new industry for a year with no traction. /s4h-network-weak-ties maps your current network and finds it's dense but homogeneous — everyone you know already knows everyone else. The entry point is a single weak tie to a different cluster, not another strong tie in your existing one.

/s4h-ecology

Your product's ecosystem of integrations looks healthy until two partners quietly announce they're building competing features. /s4h-ecology-interdependence maps the dependency web and finds three other integrations that were implicitly relying on those two partners — a cascade that wasn't visible until the dependencies were drawn out.

/s4h-aesthetic

Your apartment feels wrong but you can't articulate why. /s4h-aesthetic-coherence-check finds that three incompatible design eras are competing in the same room — and the feeling of wrongness is the parts refusing to form a whole.

/s4h-sensory

You've read the same paragraph four times and nothing is sticking. /s4h-sensory-signal-detection asks what's actually present in your environment right now that you've stopped registering — and finds three things competing for the same attention channel.
